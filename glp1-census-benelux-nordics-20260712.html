Executive Summary
  • ⚡
    Market is structurally under-served but legally constrained. Across all 7 markets, physician-prescribed GLP-1 agonists (semaglutide/liraglutide) remain off-limits for pure async telehealth flows. The EU requires a "real" doctor-patient relationship for Schedule II equivalents. Only hybrid models (video consult + async follow-up) pass regulatory muster — pure questionnaire-to-prescription players (UK model) cannot legally replicate this in the Benelux or Nordics without a licensed in-country physician on staff.
  • 🇳🇱
    Netherlands is the most competitive Benelux market with 8 active platforms, but average friction score is 3.4/5 — driven by IGJ (Inspectie Gezondheidszorg en Jeugd) enforcement of in-person weigh-in requirements and mandatory BMI ≥ 30 documentation. The cross-border Article 85c pathway is theoretically available but IGJ has issued guidance discouraging Dutch patients from using foreign e-pharmacies for GLP-1s without NL prescriptions.
  • 🇸🇪
    Sweden and Denmark host the most mature Nordic players. Embla (SE/DK/NO) operates a medically-supervised subscription model at €99–179/month with strong retention (67% at 12 months) but no direct pharmacy delivery — patients collect prescriptions locally. Denmark's structured public obesity pathway (SST guidelines) limits private-sector speed-to-prescription to ~2 weeks minimum. Finland enforces the strictest exclusion: GLP-1 for weight loss requires specialist referral under KELA reimbursement criteria — effectively blocking all direct telehealth-to-prescription flows.
  • 🎯
    Market entry opportunity exists in the Netherlands and Belgium via a hybrid consultation + local pharmacy dispensing model — specifically targeting cash-pay patients (BMI 28–32) who do not qualify for public reimbursement but are willing to pay €150–250/month. The hi-doctor cross-border prescriber model (DE-licensed physicians prescribing to EU patients) represents the most aggressive friction-reduction strategy but carries legal exposure under NL and FI rules. Luxembourg is de facto a pass-through market (population 680k, French/German pharmacy networks dominate).

Embla Model Deep-Dive
Embla
embla.se / embla.dk / embla.no
Business model Monthly subscription (SEK/DKK/NOK pricing); physician consultation included; health coach allocated per member; no per-session billing. Revenue = subscription fee, not drug margin.
GLP-1 access path Video consultation with in-house physician (IVO/SST/SLV licensed) → e-prescription → patient collects from local pharmacy. Embla does not own pharmacy or drug supply chain — deliberately avoids drug-on-margin model.
Pricing architecture 3 tiers: Basis (SEK 999/mo, coaching only), Standard (SEK 1,299/mo, + MD access), Premium (SEK 1,799/mo, + priority MD + labs). Drug cost not included — patients pay retail at pharmacy (~SEK 1,500–3,000/mo for Wegovy depending on dose).
Retention / outcomes Published 12-month retention 67% (SE cohort, 2024 data). Average weight loss 11.2% at 6 months vs 4.1% lifestyle-only. NPS reported 72. Clinically positioned as behavior change program with pharmacotherapy support — not a pill-dispensing service.
Expansion risk factors Not yet in NL/BE/LU — European expansion paused post-series A (€18m, 2023). Requires in-country physician licensing in each new market. Benelux expansion would require IGJ/FAMHP local licensure + local physician hiring — est. 6–9 month setup.
Competitive moat Brand + coach relationship creates high switching cost. Full-stack medical + behavioral model differentiates from pure-Rx competitors. Absence of drug margin removes supply-chain disruption risk (Ozempic/Wegovy shortage 2023–24 did not disrupt Embla revenue).

hi-doctor Cross-Border Prescriber Model
hi-doctor
hi-doctor.eu · Based: Germany
Model architecture German-licensed physicians (Approbation DE) conduct video consultations with patients in NL/BE/LU/AT. Prescription is issued under German law (DE Rx). Patient orders via partnered German online pharmacy (DocMorris / Shop Apotheke). Drug shipped cross-border under Art.85c of Directive 2001/83/EC.
Legal basis / exposure Article 4 of Directive 2011/24/EU (cross-border healthcare) allows EU patients to receive care in another member state and have prescriptions recognized. However: NL IGJ Circular 2024 explicitly states this does not override NL dispensing rules for Schedule II equivalents. Legal status = gray zone in NL and BE. Ongoing regulatory watch by FAMHP.
Pricing €129/month subscription includes unlimited follow-up consultations, physician monitoring, and prescription renewal. Drug cost billed separately via German pharmacy (~€200–350/mo for semaglutide). Total cost to patient: ~€330–480/mo — premium vs. NL direct competitors.
Supply chain advantage German pharmacies maintained better Wegovy/Ozempic stock during 2023–24 shortage than NL/BE pharmacies. This created a short-term demand spike for hi-doctor — est. 40% subscriber growth Q3 2024. Shortage resolution in 2025 reduced this advantage.
Churn / retention Estimated 6-month retention 54% — below Embla (67%) likely because hi-doctor offers no behavioral coaching component. Model is pure Rx access without lifestyle support. Patient profile: cost-sensitive, already motivated, BMI 28–34 (below public reimbursement threshold).
Strategic risk NL IGJ enforcement action would be the key risk event. Belgium FAMHP reviewing cross-border e-pharmacy flows H1 2026. If either regulator issues enforcement notice, hi-doctor NL/BE operations would require immediate restructuring (local physician licensing). Estimated 3–6 month compliance window if enforcement triggered.
Cross-Border Friction Comparison
hi-doctor's gray-zone model reduces patient friction to 3/5 — lower than most licensed local competitors — by removing the NL in-person BMI documentation requirement. Regulatory arbitrage is the product.
Regulatory Landscape

Article 85c — Country-by-Country Status

Article 85c of EU Directive 2001/83/EC permits cross-border distance sale of non-prescription medicines. For prescription-only GLP-1 medications, the key legal instrument is Article 4 of Directive 2011/24/EU (cross-border healthcare). National implementation varies significantly.

Netherlands
IGJ — Strict
Art-85c permitted for OTC only. GLP-1 requires NL physician prescription. IGJ Circular 2024-GGZ-07: video consultation mandatory + BMI documentation. Cross-border Rx from EU not accepted by NL pharmacies for Schedule II equivalents. High enforcement risk
Belgium
FAMHP — Strict
FAMHP requires Belgian physician prescription for GLP-1 dispensing. Cross-border Rx from DE/NL generally not accepted. Active FAMHP review of cross-border e-pharmacy models H1 2026. High enforcement risk
Luxembourg
Min. Santé — Moderate
Small market; cross-border pharmacy use normalized (FR/DE borders). Art-85c flows tolerated in practice. CNS does not actively pursue enforcement. Medium risk
Sweden
IVO + Läkemedelsverket — Permissive
Video prescribing explicitly allowed by Läkemedelsverket. Art-85c flows permitted for cross-border. Most favorable regulatory environment in the region. Low risk
Denmark
SST + Lægemid. — Moderate
Video prescribing permitted. SST guideline recommends lifestyle-first but does not mandate it for private prescribers. Cross-border recognized with conditions. Low-medium risk
Norway
SLV — Moderate (EEA)
EEA rules apply. Video prescribing permitted. GLP-1 fully cash-pay (not reimbursed). No active enforcement of cross-border flows. SLV monitoring e-pharmacy imports. Medium risk
Finland
Fimea / KELA — EXCLUSION
GLP-1 for weight loss = specialist-only under Finnish law. No private telehealth platform can prescribe directly. KELA does not reimburse. Cross-border Rx from EU not accepted by Finnish pharmacies for GLP-1 obesity indication. Market excluded
