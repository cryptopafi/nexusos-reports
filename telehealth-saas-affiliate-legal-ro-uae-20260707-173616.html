SaaS + Affiliate + Advertiser:
Telehealth Legality in Romania (EU) and UAE (Dubai/DHA)

Research by DELPHI PRO · D2 · 2026-07-07 · Illustrative context, not legal advice

Abstract

The compliant architecture: a SaaS platform that sells software to licensed healthcare providers and promotes the consultation service — not named drugs. Affiliates drive traffic to branded assessment flows; influencers disclose commercial relationships under UCPD/DSA (EU) and hold MOHAP media licences (UAE). The platform never names, recommends, or prices a prescription medicine and never intermediates a drug sale without a pharmacy licence.

Hard Red Lines:

RX peptides (BPC-157, TB-500) are unapproved in both jurisdictions — cannot be sold, marketed, or "enabled" as treatments.
EU Dir 2001/83/EC Art 88 and UAE MOHAP Ministerial Resolution 430/2007 flatly prohibit consumer-facing RX drug advertising.
Brokering a prescription sale without a licensed pharmacy partner is unlawful in both markets.

1. Scope, Caveats, and Legal Posture

Romania — Single EU entry point. Implements Directive 2001/83/EC via national law; telemedicine regulated under Law 95/2006 + GEO 8/2018 + GEO 196/2020 + GD 1133/2022.

UAE — Dubai/DHA — DHA Telehealth Standard ST-14 v4 (effective 26 November 2025); MOHAP Ministerial Resolution 430/2007; Federal Decree-Law 38/2024 on Medical Products.

US references appear only as regulatory benchmarks. They are not market-entry proposals.

2. The Core Distinction: SaaS Enabler vs Drug Marketer

2.1 The Compliant SaaS-Enabler Position

A platform is on the safe side of the line when it:

Sells software infrastructure to licensed healthcare providers (clinics, telehealth facilities, licensed physicians). The provider — not the platform — prescribes, dispenses, and takes clinical responsibility.
Promotes the service (e.g. "Get a teleconsultation with a licensed doctor") rather than naming, recommending, or pricing any specific prescription medicine.
Generates leads to a licensed provider or clinic. The patient-provider relationship is formed with the licensed party. The platform is a referral/booking conduit, not the medical actor.
Does not intermediate the drug sale — no cart, no drug pricing, no dispensing fulfilment unless a licensed pharmacy is the contracting party for that leg.

EU Legal Anchor

eCommerce Directive + DSA conditional intermediary safe harbour. MDCG 2025-4: platform hosting third-party medical apps without ownership = intermediary service provider under DSA, not MDR manufacturer. Immunity collapses if platform facilitates unlawful drug advertising.

UAE Legal Anchor

DHA ST-14 v4 distinguishes licensed health facility from technology/software providers. Platform vendor is not the licensed health facility — but must not conduct healthcare activity or drug promotion without the appropriate licence.

2.2 The Unlawful Drug-Marketer/Broker Position

A platform crosses the line when it:

Names a specific prescription medicine and promotes its use to consumers (even "Our doctors prescribe [X]" with a booking CTA is borderline; "Buy [X] online" is clearly prohibited).
Acts as a price comparison or marketplace for prescription drugs without being or partnering with a licensed pharmacy.
Enables affiliates or influencers to promote named RX medicines — even if a licensed provider is behind the curtain. The advertising act itself is the violation under Art 88.
Receives revenue as a percentage of drug sales rather than a subscription or lead fee for the service.
Named exception — unresolved: The line blurs when a platform's specialty is narrow (e.g. "peptide optimisation clinic"). If the specialty itself implies a specific unlicensed compound, even service-level copy may attract regulatory scrutiny. Requires counsel-by-counsel assessment.

3. Peptide Status: Hard Red Lines (Both Markets)

BPC-157 and TB-500 are unapproved in EU and UAE. No lawful sale as treatments exists today in either market.

EU / Romania

  • EMA has NOT issued a marketing authorisation or regulatory opinion on BPC-157 or TB-500.
  • Fall under "investigational medicinal products" requiring CTA + IMPD before any EU clinical use. MDPI Pharmaceutics (2025) confirms BPC-157 lacks the required pharmaceutical-grade characterisation.
  • Selling or supplying an unauthorised medicinal product is a criminal offence in Romania (Law 95/2006).
  • Platform cannot list "BPC-157 consultations" or "TB-500 therapy" as a service category. Doing so collapses the intermediary safe harbour.
  • WADA: BPC-157 = S0 (Non-Approved Substances); TB-500 = Growth Factor Modulator. Reputational/advertiser risk if athletes targeted.

UAE (Dubai/DHA)

  • Any product marketed for human therapeutic use without MOHAP/EDE registration = "unregistered pharmaceutical product" under Federal Decree-Law 38/2024.
  • BPC-157 and TB-500 do not appear on MOHAP's registered products list.
  • Platform cannot be the commercial front for peptide supply even if a licensed Dubai clinic is the prescriber.
  • Named exception — unresolved: Some Dubai clinics market peptide therapies under "wellness protocol" framings. Whether DHA-licensed facilities doing this operate under enforcement tolerance or formal grey zone is unclear. Counsel must confirm DHA position before enabling this category.

4. Prescription-Medicine Affiliate Marketing

4.1 EU — Directive 2001/83/EC Article 88

Art 88 prohibits advertising of prescription-only medicines (POMs) to the general public. Comprehensively harmonised across EU (ECJ C-374/05 Gintec, 2007). Applies to social media and digital channels. EU Pharma Package revision expected to maintain and expand this prohibition (~2028-2029 effective date).

All advertising of medicines requires prior MOHAP approval. Influencers need: (1) UAE Media Council commercial licence; (2) MOHAP healthcare advertising licence (AED 1,000/month or AED 3,000/year); (3) DHA Medical Director approval for facility-branded content.

5. Influencer Disclosure Regimes

EU (applies in Romania)

  • UCPD (Dir 2005/29/EC): Applies whenever influencer receives ANY consideration — monetary, free product, affiliate %. Disclosure must be visible and prominent. Brand/platform co-liable even without editorial control.
  • DSA (Reg 2022/2065): Art 26 requires platforms to label commercial communications in real time. Art 39 requires VLOPs to maintain ad repository — creates audit trail.
  • Digital Fairness Act (proposed 2025): Would codify influencer disclosure more explicitly. Still in legislative process. Plan for tightening.
  • Romania: UCPD via Law 363/2007. No additional influencer licensing analogous to UAE. AVMSD baseline applies.
  • Generic hashtag piles ("#ad" buried in many tags) may be insufficient — disclosure must be impossible to miss per EU guidance.

UAE (Dubai/DHA)

  • Media Council influencer licence mandatory for commercial promotions.
  • DHA ST-21 (2022): Official account must display MOHAP medical advertisement licence number.
  • Healthcare professionals must use only DHA-licensed specialty title. "Anti-aging specialist," "aesthetic consultant," "beauty expert" explicitly prohibited.
  • All filmed content within health facility premises — including personal mobile devices — is the facility's liability.
  • Content must present advantages AND disadvantages. Absolute statements and alarming expressions prohibited.
  • Named exception — unresolved: Interaction between Media Council and MOHAP approval when a non-UAE influencer promotes a UAE platform to a UAE audience is not resolved in public guidance.

6. Advertiser-Network Constraints

Meta (Facebook + Instagram)

  • From Jan 2025: EU healthcare advertisers cannot use Meta Pixel, custom audiences from website behaviour, or lookalike audiences built from health visitors (GDPR + DSA).
  • Lower-funnel conversion events (Purchase, Add to Cart, Appointment Booking) restricted or blocked for EU healthcare advertisers.
  • Online pharmacies and pharmaceutical advertisers must be LegitScript certified.
  • What can still run (EU): brand awareness campaigns for the telehealth service (not naming drugs); traffic campaigns to booking page; lead ads with strict data minimisation.
  • UAE-targeted campaigns face fewer tracking restrictions but MOHAP pre-approval of content is the advertiser's legal obligation — Meta does not enforce it at the platform level.

Google Ads

  • Pharmaceutical manufacturers must be Google-certified (LegitScript as prerequisite for many categories).
  • Telemedicine is a specific Google certification category — separate from Online Pharmacy. Requires demonstrating the underlying licensed provider network.
  • Prescription drug sales to consumers prohibited unless advertiser holds country-specific pharmacy licence + Google certification.
  • OTC medicines with Google certification allowed, subject to age-gating.

TikTok

  • Prescription medicines: NOT allowed globally. No certification pathway exists.
  • OTC medicines: conditionally allowed with national authority proof (MOHAP/UAE, ANMDM/Romania); must age-gate to 18+.
  • Medical institutions/clinics: conditionally allowed with Ministry of Health registration + facility licence proof.
  • Platform can promote telehealth service under "medical institution" category if underlying licensed clinic provides licence documentation. Named drugs or peptides cannot appear.

Key constraint across all three platforms

The underlying licensed entity — not the SaaS layer — must hold the relevant certification or licence. The platform can advertise in co-branding with its licensed provider partners if those providers are certified. The platform cannot hold certifications for providers it merely enables.

7. The Broker Model: Lawful vs Unlawful Forms

Lawful Forms

A. Appointment marketplace

Platform lists DHA-licensed (UAE) or ANMDMR-registered (Romania) providers. Patient books consultation; fee goes to clinic; platform takes booking commission. Platform must not exercise clinical control; provider retains prescribing authority and liability.

B. Lead-generation to licensed clinic

Affiliate drives a lead to the licensed clinic. Clinic pays flat lead fee or % of consultation revenue (NOT of drug revenue). Content used to generate the lead must not name or promote specific RX medicines.

C. Pharmacy referral (OTC only)

Licensed pharmacy (ANMDM in Romania; DHA/DOH/MOHAP in UAE) is the selling party. Platform earns software licence fee or % of OTC product revenue. Marketing copy promotes the pharmacy service, not specific RX drugs.

Unlawful Forms

Platform sells RX drugs directly without holding a pharmacy licence — illegal in both markets.
Platform enables purchasing of unlicensed compounds (BPC-157, TB-500) — illegal in both markets under pharmaceutical products law.
Platform receives revenue as % of RX drug dispensing without a licensed pharmacy as contracting entity.
Cross-border prescription fulfilment where a Romanian provider prescribes and a non-EU pharmacy fulfils — GDPR + pharmaceutical law + customs complexity. Do not operationalise without multi-jurisdiction counsel.

10. Named Exceptions and Unresolved Items

These items carry material uncertainty. They cannot be resolved from public sources alone and must be flagged to counsel before commercial decisions.

1. Dubai peptide grey zone

Whether DHA-licensed clinics administering BPC-157/TB-500 under physician supervision operate within enforcement tolerance or formal regulatory pathway is unclear. If the platform lists such clinics, it may share in the legal exposure.

2. Narrow-specialty copy problem

When a platform's specialty is implicitly linked to an unapproved compound (e.g. "peptide optimisation clinic"), even service-level ad copy may attract scrutiny as implied drug promotion. Threshold not defined in current EU or UAE guidance.

3. UAE cross-market influencer reach

Whether UAE Media Council and MOHAP licensing requirements apply extraterritorially to a non-UAE influencer promoting a UAE platform to a UAE audience is unresolved. Seek formal MOHAP position.

4. Revenue-share vs fixed-fee affiliate (Romania)

Whether an affiliate earning % of total order value (where order includes OTC products post-consultation) triggers pharmaceutical revenue-sharing rules under Romanian law is not addressed in ANMDMR guidance. Fixed consultation fees are safer.

5. EU Digital Fairness Act timeline

DFA still in legislative process (2025-2026). Final scope on affiliate commission disclosure and influencer obligations may affect platform affiliate T&Cs. Monitor.

6. Google Telemedicine Certification — linked to provider licence

Google requires demonstration of the licensed provider network. If the platform launches before its provider roster holds national licences, the certification application will fail. Timeline-dependent risk.

7. Romania telemedicine prescription rules (GD 1133/2022)

Whether a Romanian telehealth consultation can legally result in an electronic prescription for a controlled substance (e.g. scheduled hormones) under GD 1133/2022 requires direct counsel review.

11. Synthesis: The Compliant Architecture

Platform Layer

  • Sells software subscriptions to licensed telehealth providers (DHA-licensed in UAE; ANMDMR-registered in Romania)
  • Does not prescribe, dispense, or name specific medicines
  • Earns SaaS subscription or booking fee — not pharmaceutical revenue
  • Data hosted in UAE for UAE clients (AED 700k fine risk); GDPR-compliant for EU clients
  • If platform software meets MDR MDSW definition, CE marking required; MDCG 2025-4 intermediary exemption may apply for booking/CRM layer — requires legal analysis

Affiliate/Influencer Layer

  • EU: Affiliates promote consultation brand and booking CTA; no named medicines; full UCPD disclosure; platform co-liable for influencer non-disclosure
  • UAE: Influencers hold Media Council licence + MOHAP healthcare advertising licence; MOHAP pre-approves each content piece; DHA Medical Director approves facility-associated content
  • Revenue model: flat lead fee or % of consultation fee — not % of drug revenue
  • No affiliate or influencer may name, imply, or link to BPC-157, TB-500, or any unapproved compound in any market

Advertiser Layer

  • Meta EU: awareness and traffic campaigns only; no pixel-based retargeting; no health data in custom audiences; LegitScript for any pharmacy partner
  • Google: Telemedicine certification required; tied to provider network build timeline
  • TikTok: Promote as medical institution (with licence proof); no RX drugs or peptides
  • All ad copy promotes the service ("speak to a licensed doctor") — not a medicine

Broker/Intermediary Layer

  • Appointment marketplace: licensed providers list services; patient pays clinic; platform takes booking commission
  • Pharmacy referral (OTC only): licensed pharmacy is the selling entity; platform is booking/referral infrastructure
  • Peptide services: do not list until both markets confirm a lawful pathway — currently unresolved in Romania/EU and likely non-compliant in UAE without MOHAP product registration
