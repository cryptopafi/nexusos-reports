Greece → Romania:
Cross-Border Rx Delivery

Can a Greek pharmacy lawfully mail prescription medicines to Romanian patients?

⛔ Executive Verdict — Negative
Mail-order of prescription-only medicines from Greece to Romanian patients is not lawful under any existing EU or national mechanism.

The arrangement fails at three independent legal layers. Each is individually sufficient to block it.

Three Independent Blocking Layers

1
EU sending-side: Directive 2011/62/EU Article 85c(3) — member states may restrict or prohibit distance-selling of POM (prescription-only medicines). Greece has exercised this restriction.
2
Greece national: Greek online pharmacy framework (via 2016 ministerial circular) was not substantially activated for POM as of 2022 and remains inactive for outbound cross-border mail-order. No cross-border outbound POM mail provision identified.
3
Romania receiving-side: Romanian Law of Pharmacy 266/2008 + ANMDM: dispensing occurs only through registered community pharmacies. “Buying prescription medicines from unregistered foreign online sources is illegal.” (ANMDM official; ExpatFocus Romania 2025)
⚠ Note on Greek Pharmacy Verbal Claim

The verbal claim from the Greek pharmacy (“we can deliver to Romania”) most likely reflects either (a) misreading of 2011/24/EU prescription recognition (which covers in-person dispensing, not mail-order), (b) reference to OTC products only, or (c) an informal/grey-market arrangement. It does not reflect a verified lawful export mechanism for POM.

✔ Viable Architecture: Route 2
Greek-licensed tele-doctor → e-prescription → ANMDM-registered Romanian pharmacy → Romanian patient

A viable lawful route exists via a different architecture. Greek-licensed telemedicine physician issues an EU-format e-prescription. The Romanian patient presents it at an ANMDM-registered Romanian pharmacy (in-person or domestic online delivery within Romania). This is Route 2 and is the commercially viable, SaaS-enabler-compatible architecture.

01 Greek Pharmacy Cross-Border Dispensing Law

The Controlling EU Instrument: Directive 2011/62/EU

A precision error common in cross-border pharmacy discussions is attributing the mail-order framework to Directive 2011/24/EU (patient rights). The operative instrument governing online pharmacy distance-selling is Directive 2011/62/EU (Falsified Medicines Directive), specifically Article 85c(3):

“Member States may restrict or prohibit the distance selling of medicinal products subject to prescription.”

This is an explicit opt-out. Each EU member state decides whether to permit or prohibit mail-order of POM. There is no EU-wide mandate that pharmacies must (or may) mail prescription medicines across borders.

Greek Implementation and EOF Status

Greece transposed the online pharmacy framework via Ministerial Circular Γ5(β)/Γ.Π.οικ.20293/2016. A licensed Greek pharmacy may apply to operate an online store through the Hellenic Pharmaceutical Association (ΠΦΣ).

Critical Finding

The Greek National Transparency Authority (EAD) audit (September 2021 – February 2022, covering 250+ websites) found that the online pharmacy provision “had not been substantially activated, due to an institutional request by the ΠΦΣ for the establishment of restrictions on categories of medicines that could be sold via the internet.” The 2026 IBA Healthcare Survey confirms no cross-border outbound POM mail-order provision was identified.

Net Greek position: No lawful basis found for a Greek pharmacy to mail POM (sildenafil, tadalafil, semaglutide, tirzepatide) to a Romanian patient. Cross-border outbound mail-order for POM is not an activated, authorized category.

Counsel gate: An EOF circular dated 2023–2026 could theoretically have updated this position. No such circular was identified in this research. Greek pharmaceutical counsel should confirm current EOF stance before any commercial decision.

There is no lawful mechanism by which a Greek pharmacy can mail POM directly to a Romanian patient under current EU and national law.

03 Romania’s Receiving-Side Rules: Is the Ban Symmetric?

Romanian Law of Pharmacy No. 266/2008 (ANMDM official): Pharmaceutical assistance is ensured through community pharmacies and drugstores. Prescription medicine dispensing occurs through registered community pharmacies only. There is no provision for dispensing by foreign pharmacies to Romanian residents.

ANMDM Official Position

“Buying prescription medicines from unregistered foreign online sources is illegal and poses genuine health risks.” (ANMDM website; ExpatFocus Romania 2025)

The Ban is Structural, Not Just Operator-Level

The Romania ban operates at multiple independent points:

  1. The sending pharmacy has no authorisation to mail POM to Romanian residents (no Romanian wholesale/dispensing licence)
  2. ANMDM does not authorise foreign pharmacies to supply Romanian patients remotely
  3. Romanian customs/postal regulations: prescription medicines from non-authorised foreign pharmacies can be seized
  4. The individual patient receiving such a shipment is not protected by any Romanian legal safe harbour
Key Distinction from NL→RO Question

The NL→Romania finding in the v4 model was correct. The Netherlands' permissive domestic mail-order framework does not help because Romania's receiving-side rules block the delivery regardless of the sending country. Greece→Romania faces the identical Romania-side wall. The sending country (NL vs GR) makes no difference if Romania does not authorize inbound foreign pharmacy deliveries. There is no legal distinction that makes Greece→Romania delivery lawful where NL→Romania was flagged as blocked.

04 Greece as Doctor / Telemedicine Base for Romanian Patients

Greece’s New Telemedicine Legal Framework (2024–2025)

NEW
Law 5129/2024, Article 66 — Greece's first explicit telemedicine legalisation. Formally authorises the use of digital applications for medical examination and procedures, amending the Code of Medical Ethics (Law 3418/2005).
NEW
Ministerial Decision YA 7686/2025 (ΦΕΚ 1083/Β/10-3-2025) — March 2025 decision establishing terms, conditions, and procedures for operating a telemedicine application.
TAX
E.2008/2026 (Greek Tax Authority, Feb 2026) — VAT-exempt confirmation for telemedicine services with therapeutic purpose (diagnosis, treatment, prevention). Equivalent to in-person medical services.

Route 2: The Viable Architecture

✔ Viable: Route 2 Architecture

A Greek-licensed physician may conduct telemedicine consultations and issue e-prescriptions digitally under Law 5129/2024. The Romanian patient can then present the EU cross-border format prescription (Directive 2012/52/EU: patient name, DoB, prescriber details, INN, format, strength, dosage, signature) at:

  1. A physical Romanian pharmacy (in-person, lawful under 2011/24/EU)
  2. An ANMDM-registered Romanian online pharmacy (domestic delivery within Romania, lawful if the pharmacy is registered)

Language Considerations

Greek medical practice is primarily in Greek. For a Romania-first patient base, a Greek doctor network would need to operate in Romanian or English. This is a practical, not legal, constraint. The cross-border prescription format is language-neutral (uses INN generic names). This is an operational hurdle, not a legal blocker.

What Greece Adds to the v4 Model

  1. Greek doctor network as medical consultation layer — Law 5129/2024 + YA 7686/2025 gives legal cover from March 2025.
  2. 22% CIT and VAT-exempt telemedicine — if the corporate entity for medical services is Greek-registered, this may reduce tax burden on the consultation revenue stream.
  3. Proximity arbitrage — Greece is geographically and culturally closer to Romania/Balkans; potentially easier to recruit bilingual doctors and establish patient trust.

What Greece does NOT add: Any pharmacy fulfillment advantage. The mail-order gap is a Romania-side block, not a sending-country variable.

Whether the Medical Association has guidance or restrictions on cross-border telemedicine prescribing. Gate: Greek Medical Association inquiry.

GR-006 MEDIUM complexity
