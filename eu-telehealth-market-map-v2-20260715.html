1. Executive Summary

No vendor is simultaneously (a) EU-native/EU-hosted, (b) headless/API-first, and (c) turnkey on e-prescription + pharmacy fulfilment. This is a COMPOSE play, not a license-one-vendor play.

Every EU-native orchestration layer (Awell, Alakin) stops short of pharmacy fulfilment; every turnkey pharmacy rail (DocMorris/Redcare, REMPe/Delonia) is a closed national system, not an API-first platform; every architecturally-close full-stack analog (Wheel, Fabric, Beluga, Canvas) is US-only and cannot be deployed for EU patients as-is. Budget and timeline should assume integration of 4-6 independent vendors/rails plus meaningful in-house glue code (orchestration state machine, per-country pharmacy connectors, clinician review UI).

Recommended buy-vs-build split by layer
  • BUY (commodity): video/telecom (Vonage Video EU/DE zones, or LiveKit self-hosted), payments, identity/KYC (Nect for DE-first, Signicat as pan-EU broker).
  • BUY/INTEGRATE, choose carefully: FHIR EHR backbone — pick FHIR-native (Firely Server EU-native, or Medplum self-hosted-EU) specifically to avoid a 2028-29 EHDS interoperability migration.
  • BUY per-country, budget 4-9 months per market: e-prescription + pharmacy fulfilment — no pan-EU rail exists; start Germany, add France, then Benelux/Nordics.
  • BUILD in-house (the differentiator, and the regulatory-control point): clinical eligibility/triage logic and the async→review→titration orchestration state machine, kept clinician-assistive (not autonomous) to stay outside MDR Class IIa/AI Act high-risk classification where possible — orchestrate on Awell (EU-native) or build directly on Medplum Bots.
  • Timeline: ~4-6 months to a single-market (DE) pilot; 9-12+ months to a 3-5 market EU footprint, gated primarily by per-country eRx/pharmacy re-certification and national medical-board sign-off, not by technology.

5. EU Regulatory Constraints

EHDS — Regulation (EU) 2025/327 (European Health Data Space)

Adopted 11 Feb 2025, published in the Official Journal 5 Mar 2025 (CELEX 32025R0327). Date conflict flagged: secondary sources disagree on the in-force date — some state 25 Mar 2025, others 26 May 2025. This report defers to the EUR-Lex Official Journal date and marks the discrepancy as an open item for counsel (see Section 9) rather than averaging or guessing. Substantively: EEHRxF (European Electronic Health Record exchange format) FHIR specifications due 26 Mar 2027; primary-use interoperability obligations (patient summaries + ePrescriptions) go live Mar 2029; imaging/labs follow Mar 2031. Practical implication: an operator launching before 2029 has no FHIR mandate yet under EHDS — choosing FHIR-native infrastructure now (Section C2) is a future-proofing choice, not a current legal requirement.

MDR (EU) 2017/745 + MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1 (published 17 Jun 2025)

Covers modular medical device software (MDSW) classification, with a cross-reference to AI Act "MDAI" subsets, and the "medical purpose" test. GLP-1 eligibility-screening logic sits in a gray zone: arguably OUTSIDE MDR scope if it is a static criteria checklist (BMI thresholds, contraindication flags) reviewed by a clinician before action; it risks classification as Class I or IIa if it autonomously recommends dose or drug without clinician review. This is a case-by-case determination requiring a specific counsel opinion on the operator's actual algorithm (see Section 9).

AI Act — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689

In force Aug 2024; prohibitions applied Feb 2025; GPAI obligations Aug 2025; full high-risk obligations apply Aug 2026. Annex III(5)(a) classifies medical-device AI / CDS systems as high-risk where they gate access to care — meaning Aug 2026 is a real compliance deadline for any AI-driven triage or eligibility layer the operator builds or buys.

MyHealth@EU

Government-to-government (G2G) infrastructure only. HIGH confidence — but note per fix #4: this is inferred from the consistent architecture of EU Commission / NCPeH FHIR Implementation Guide documentation (health.ec.europa.eu eHealth, build-fhir.ehdsi.eu/ncp-api) describing a gateway scoped to national contact points — not based on a single explicit "no commercial API" statement from the Commission. Treat it as unusable for private-sector integration.

Member-state GLP-1 prescribing friction (patchwork, not harmonized)
  • Germany: Fernbehandlung permitted where the remote medium is "medically justifiable" — pushes toward requiring an initial sync/video visit for new weight-management prescriptions MEDIUM.
  • France: Teleconsultation legally equal to in-person, but practically a physical exam expected for new patients; as of 15 Jun 2026, reimbursed first-prescription of Wegovy/Mounjaro is reserved to specialized obesity centers (CSO), university hospitals, or endocrinology MEDIUM-HIGH — this materially narrows the reimbursed-patient addressable market in France.
  • Netherlands: BIG-registered doctor required, remote practice used, but the async-only restriction is unclear LOW-MEDIUM — gap, needs local counsel.
  • Ireland: IMC-registered doctor + ongoing oversight required; specifics unclear LOW-MEDIUM gap.
  • Spain, Sweden, Denmark: async-vs-sync prescribing rule not found in this research pass UNVERIFIED — gap.
  • Pattern: no EU-wide ban on async prescribing, but Germany and France in particular create real friction for a zero-sync, text-only model — plan for at least one synchronous touchpoint in the largest markets.

6. Practitioner & Buyer Sentiment (new in v2 — social lanes)

Seven corroborated (2+ independent source) pain points surfaced from HN, Reddit-proxy, and trade press:

  1. Twilio Video migration noise — real pain among teams that had already begun migrating off Twilio before the Oct 2024 reversal (HN + 4 trade-press pieces). Per fix #1, the underlying EOL threat is resolved, but the noise is evidence of roadmap-confidence residue to price in.
  2. eRezept / DocMorris friction — Germany's e-prescription pharmacy pipeline described as "technisch sehr holprig" (technically very bumpy) by the president of the German pharmacists' association; DocMorris stock fell ~20% over 3 months (Aug 2025) with analysts citing eRezept problems; DocMorris + Shop Apotheke filed an EU Commission complaint over the eGK-chip redemption flow; app-store login/transfer friction reported across 5+ sources.
  3. Identity-verification (IDV) sales-gating — Onfido/Veriff pricing gated behind sales calls, with $50-200K/yr enterprise minimums and weeks-long sandbox onboarding (HN + G2 + vendor sites).
  4. FHIR promise-vs-reality gap — multiple r/healthIT threads describe systems still running on HL7 v2 underneath FHIR marketing, with one specific complaint of "$20,000 fee each time someone wants to connect."
  5. Daily.co pricing objections — reported cost of ~$10K/mo at 1,000 users × 2hrs driving teams toward self-hosted LiveKit/Jitsi instead (2 independent HN threads).
  6. Kry's Germany exit (2022, 400 layoffs) post-DVG-era over-optimism (Sifted reporting) — a cautionary tale for over-scaling ahead of reimbursement clarity.
  7. Doctolib support degradation past tier-1 — corroborated across separate r/germany and r/france national threads, alongside resentment of the €100-150/mo/doctor price point and a €4.6M French antitrust fine.

7. Shortlist — 5 Most Viable Build-On Options

1. Awell Health (orchestration layer)

The only EU-native (Brussels), genuinely API-first (GraphQL + FHIR/HL7 bridge) care-flow engine that natively models the async-questionnaire→clinician-review state machine. Survives because nothing else combines EU jurisdiction with headless orchestration at this maturity.

2. Firely Server (FHIR system-of-record)

EU-native (Amsterdam), maintains the OSS Firely SDK, already engaged with EHDS/MedMij, no US CLOUD Act exposure. Cleanest data-sovereignty foundation, ahead of Medplum for teams wanting EU jurisdiction baked in rather than self-managed.

3. Vonage Video (video/telecom)

The only video API with concrete, named EU and Germany-only Regional Media Zones plus a credible enterprise parent (Ericsson) and a HIPAA-BAA path. Survives on data-residency specificity that competitors only gesture at.

4. Infermedica (triage/CDS)

Class IIb MDR classification with a three-body certification stack (Dekra + TÜV Nord), a genuinely open developer API, and native Arabic-language support for adjacent markets. Highest-assurance triage engine with real API access (vs. Ada's gated partner-only model).

5. DocMorris/Redcare (pharmacy fulfilment)

The most mature single-vendor e-prescription-to-pharmacy rail spanning DE/AT/NL/FR/IT/BE, with Redcare's ISO 27001 certified by TÜV Rheinland. The only pharmacy partner with multi-country reach today, even though it is not a true pan-EU API.

Runners-up worth tracking: Alakin/Tech2Heal for its EU-sovereign-cloud story if its funding runway clears diligence; Medplum as the lower-lock-in FHIR alternative; Nect for gematik-native identity if launching DE-first; LiveKit if the team accepts self-host DevOps overhead over Vonage's managed zones.
