2 Final Top 5 Ranking (Ease + Time-to-Operational)

Ranking weights evidenced incorporation speed + known operational friction. UNSCORED jurisdictions cannot climb on unproven speed.

#1
Malta No tension
Fastest evidenced incorporation (1–2 wks [T2]) + lightest known licensing posture — no dedicated telehealth law, consulting-room exclusion may spare virtual-only operators [T1]. Operational axis UNSCORED (HCSD timeline not found) but rank rests on best incorporation speed + lowest known burden. Coincides with compliance #1 — no divergence.
#2
Portugal ⚠ Tension: compliance #4
5-day express LDA (Empresa na Hora [T2]) = single fastest formation data point in the set. Outranks Estonia/Netherlands on incorporation ease. Tension for counsel: do NOT read as compliance promotion — ERS licensing timeline is UNSCORED, banking evidence is thin (one T2 source). Ease advantage is real; operational advantage is unconfirmed.
#3
Estonia Banking drag
Best-in-class OÜ registration (1 business day) but 2–8 wk e-Residency pre-requisite + High non-resident banking friction (traditional bank restrictions, rejection risk [T2]) pull it below its compliance rank #2. Prescriber timelines are the only quantified ones (non-EU: 3 mo + 1 mo [T1]). Banking friction is the critical path item to de-risk before committing.
#4
Cyprus Consistent with compliance #5
Clean 7–10 business-day formation [T2, two consistent sources], but High banking friction ("most challenging part of setup" [T2], no numeric estimate) and hard prescriber gate (non-EU doctors = 3 years experience in recognizing Member State [T1]) cap operational speed. No upward tension vs compliance ranking.
#5
Netherlands ⬇ Inverts: compliance #3
Lowest combined ease+time despite compliance rank #3. Only jurisdiction with two quantified adverse operational facts: 8-week Wtza licence [T1 KVK.nl] + prescribing prohibition (never-physically-met, narrowed by April 2023 exception [T2 cms.law]). Highest known licensing burden = slowest realistic time-to-first-lawful-consult.

3 Reconciliation with 2026-07-11 Compliance Ranking

Malta holds #1 on both axes. Compliance (LN 316/2021 + cleanest licence) and ease/time (fastest evidenced formation, lightest known burden) agree — strongest signal in the dataset for a Malta hub.
Netherlands inverts: compliance #3 → ease/time #5. The 8-week Wtza licence [T1] + never-physically-met prescribing prohibition [T2] make it the slowest-to-operational primary. Compliance strength does not equal speed to first consult.
Portugal inverts upward on ease: compliance #4 → ease/time #2 on 5-day formation strength. Flagged tension for counsel — do not promote. ERS licensing timeline UNSCORED; banking evidence thin.
Estonia's banking drag is the gap between compliance strength (#2, best e-health/CBeP) and operational reality (High banking friction). Malta/Estonia hub decision must budget Estonian non-resident banking as a live risk.
Cyprus is consistent (low on both), reinforcing fallback status, not primary hub.
Net for Malta/Estonia hub decision: Malta is the only jurisdiction with no ease/time-vs-compliance tension → primary hub candidate. Estonia remains the CBeP/e-prescription layer but banking friction is the critical path item.
Both orderings answer different questions (licensing clarity vs. speed to operational); divergence is expected. Hub choice should optimize Malta-first with Estonia as the CBeP/e-prescription layer.
