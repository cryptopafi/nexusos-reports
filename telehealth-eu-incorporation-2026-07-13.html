Executive Summary

Final Ranking (Ease + Time-to-Operational): #1 Portugal  ·  #2 Malta  ·  #3 Netherlands  ·  #4 Cyprus  ·  #5 Estonia

Portugal (#1) — 5-business-day express formation (Empresa na Hora) atop an established, public ERS licensing framework. Banking and prescriber timelines UNSCORED but formation speed is the best-evidenced of any candidate.

Malta (#2) — 1-2 week formation. T1 HCSD Manual (March 2024) strongly implies virtual-only telehealth is exempt from the physical-clinic licence. Critical gap: LN 316/2021 text was never retrieved and could override this inference.

Netherlands (#3) — BV entity in 1-2 weeks but 3-6 months all-in to operational. Active IGJ enforcement investigation targets the exact async/bundled GLP-1 model contemplated here.

Cyprus (#4) — 7-10 day formation, but "most challenging" banking friction and a 3-year EU-experience gate on non-EU prescribers.

Estonia (#5 — Structural Disqualifier) — Foreign-owned e-Residency entities CANNOT hold Estonian healthcare activity licences. The 1-day OÜ headline is operationally irrelevant.

Universal caveat: Formation speed and operational readiness are different clocks. Every ranking cell rests on unretrieved or unconfirmed licensing timelines that MUST be verified by jurisdiction-specific legal counsel before capital deployment. This report is decision-support, not legal advice.

#5 — Estonia (Structural Disqualifier)
Ease 4/10 · Foreign-Owned Entity NOT Licence-Eligible
Compliance rank: #2
-3 collapse on ease axis

Plain words: Estonia is famous for one-day company formation and world-class digital health infrastructure. For a foreign-owned telehealth platform this is a trap: the law only lets genuinely domestic Estonian companies hold a healthcare licence, so the fast e-Residency OÜ cannot be licensed to operate the clinical service.

STRUCTURAL DISQUALIFIER — Contradiction C1

Only domestic (Estonian-organised) companies can hold healthcare activity licences. Foreign-owned e-Residency OÜ entities are NOT eligible. Source: IBA Healthcare Survey Estonia / TGS Baltic, Nov 2022 T2.

The "1-day OÜ" headline is technically accurate but operationally irrelevant: (a) e-Residency card takes 2-8 weeks; (b) resulting entity cannot hold the licence; (c) even with a genuine domestic company, the activity-licence processing timeline is NOT FOUND in any retrieved source; (d) non-EU prescriber registration = 4 months (3mo review + 1mo post-exam) T1.

Must integrate with national Health Information System T2
Processing timeline: NOT FOUND in any source

Preserved Contradictions (Critic-Identified)

C1 — Estonia: "1 day OÜ" vs structural disqualifier
1-day OÜ formation is technically true but operationally irrelevant: (a) e-Residency pre-req = 2-8 weeks; (b) resulting foreign-owned entity CANNOT hold the healthcare activity licence; (c) even with genuinely domestic entity, processing timeline unknown. Resolution: Estonia Axis B = UNSCORED + disqualifier flagged as go/no-go item.
C2 — Netherlands: entity 1-2 weeks vs all-in 3-6 months
Different endpoints; both correct. Entity-only BV registration = 1-2 weeks. All-in (entity + bank + Wtza + operational prescribing infrastructure) = 3-6 months, ~€10-20k. Always specify which endpoint is meant.
C3 — Portugal: 5 business days vs 2-6 weeks
Express Empresa na Hora = 5 business days (standardised articles; eligibility constraints apply). Standard notarised route = 2-6 weeks. Both valid and both conditional on entity type and ownership complexity.
C4 — Malta: virtual-only likely exempt vs LN 316/2021 text not retrieved
T1 HCSD Manual (March 2024) supports the exemption inference via physical-premises requirement and advisory-room exclusion. LN 316/2021 existence confirmed; full text never retrieved; could reinforce or override. Not resolvable without retrieval and solicitor review.
C5 — Portugal: compliance rank #4 vs ease rank #1
Portugal's 5-day express formation earns it ease rank #1. Its compliance posture (#4 in the Jul 11 ranking) reflects licensing framework concerns unrelated to formation speed. Axes are intentionally separate; never use ease rank to promote compliance inference.
